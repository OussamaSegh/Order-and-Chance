Learn to prove, to calculate, to solve problems, and to think like a mathematician.
Every journey in mathematics starts with the same tools, and this book sharpens them until they are a pleasure to use.
It begins with mathematical language and proof, then develops sums and induction, counting, limits and series,
complex numbers and roots of unity, polynomials, linear algebra, calculus, inequalities and first probability. Each chapter opens with
the story of the people who found these ideas, rebuilds its tools from scratch, and then develops proofs of the
intermediate value theorem, Stirling's formula, Cayley--Hamilton, the spectral theorem, the law of large numbers.
Each tool comes with the problems that make it shine: roots of unity that turn a sum of binomial coefficients into a picture,
one inequality between means that settles a dozen optimisation problems, a determinant that measures how a map stretches
volume, a random surfer who ranks the pages of the web. Exercises are central to the book, graded from warm-ups to gems,
each with a hint and a complete solution that first explains how to find the idea and only then writes it down.
Pause-and-think boxes ask you to guess before you read on, challenge boxes open doors to the research frontier. Guided workshops turn false patterns, runaway robots, uncertain tests and imperfect data into problems with staged hints and complete solutions. A field guide helps readers choose their next tool.
