\chapter*{About the Cover}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{About the Cover}
\noindent The curve on which y squared equals x cubed minus x plus one, a pencil of nearby level curves, and the straight chord through two points of the curve, meeting it a third time and reflected to the sum. That chord-and-tangent rule is the group law. Niels Abel and Carl Jacobi built the analytic theory of elliptic functions in the 1820s. Louis Mordell proved in 1922 that the rational points of such a curve form a finitely generated group. The part Elliptic Curves and Modular Forms is this volume's account of that arithmetic.
