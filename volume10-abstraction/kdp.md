# Description

The number 6 factors as 2 × 3 and as (1 + √−5)(1 − √−5). In the ring of numbers a + b√−5, these are genuinely different factorizations. Prime ideals restore uniqueness. Why do they succeed where numbers fail?

Volume 10 of Order and Chance follows the maps between algebraic structures. Categories, functors and universal properties describe products, free objects and adjoints. Concrete tensors lead to Kronecker products, matrix equations and Strassen's algorithm. Tensor products of modules and the snake lemma then make exact sequences into working tools.

Hilbert's basis theorem and Nullstellensatz connect systems of polynomial equations with ideals. Localisation studies a ring near one prime. Dimension, primary decomposition and Dedekind domains prepare the ideal theory used in algebraic number theory. Projective resolutions, Tor and Ext measure failures of exactness, while group cohomology classifies extensions and explains carrying in ordinary addition.

The final three chapters develop representation theory. Wedderburn's theorem, induction, Mackey's formula and character arguments lead to Burnside's theorem. Specht modules and Young tableaux describe the symmetric group, with proofs of the hook-length formula and Robinson–Schensted correspondence. For compact matrix groups, Peter–Weyl gives Fourier completeness. Weyl's character formula for U(n) then turns trace moments into counts of permutations with a bound on their longest increasing subsequence.

Small examples precede abstract arguments where they help. Theorems on the main path come with proofs; further quoted results are identified explicitly. Each chapter has graded exercises, hints and full solutions in idea/writeup form.

The book assumes the algebra and finite-group characters of Volume 3, topology and integration from Volume 7, Hilbert-space methods from Volume 8, and the geometry and homology of Volume 9. It prepares the representation calculations used for shuffling in Volume 11 and the Dedekind-domain theory applied in Volume 13.
