# Description

Rotate a square, shuffle four cards, or exchange two roots of a polynomial. Which changes preserve the relations between the objects? That question leads from concrete symmetries to groups, rings and fields.

One route leads to Galois theory: why radical formulas solve polynomial equations over the rationals through degree four, yet cannot express a root of x⁵ − 4x + 2 starting from rational numbers. Another turns groups into matrices. The irreducible representations of the cube's rotation group explain the identity 24 = 1² + 1² + 2² + 3² + 3². Character tables record the traces of these representations and make counting and symmetry visible in a new form.

On the way, count necklaces up to rotation, prove the attainability criterion for the 15-puzzle, and use Sylow's theorems to study finite groups. Ideals and modules connect integer and polynomial arithmetic to matrix normal forms. Field extensions show why a 60-degree angle cannot be trisected with ruler and compass; finite fields recover damaged messages. Quaternions bring the story back to rotations in three dimensions. Four appendices develop positive matrices, spectral counting, polynomial methods and finite-field Hadamard constructions, with their needed arguments supplied locally.

The developed theory comes with complete proofs. Marked research outlooks point beyond it. Small examples motivate the abstractions, and every graded exercise has a hint and a complete solution beginning with how to find the idea. Figures and sources for further reading accompany the chapters.

For readers with high-school mathematics and the material developed in Volumes 1 and 2 of Order and Chance.
