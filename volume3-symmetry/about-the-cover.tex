\chapter*{About the Cover}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{About the Cover}
\noindent The cover shows a regular icosahedron: twelve vertices, thirty edges and twenty triangular faces. Three pale lines mark rotation axes through opposite vertices, faces and edges; the corresponding rotations have orders five, three and two. The faint circles are the intersections of its mirror planes with the surrounding sphere, projected into the picture. Euclid constructs the solid in Book XIII, Proposition 16 of the Elements. Here its sixty rotations lead to the alternating group on five letters, proved in the closing chapter, A Glimpse Beyond: Quaternions and Rotations. That group also appears in the obstruction to solving quintic equations by radicals; the connection requires the Galois theory developed earlier in this volume.
