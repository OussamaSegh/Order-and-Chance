Algebra is the study of what survives a transformation. This book builds groups, rings, fields and representations
from the single idea of symmetry, and climbs to two summits. The first is Galois's answer to a three-hundred-year-old
question: there is a formula in radicals for the roots of every equation of degree four, but none for
$x^5-4x+2=0$, because a group of permutations of its roots is too large to be built from abelian pieces. The second is
the character table of a finite group, a small square of numbers that holds most of what there is to know about the
group. On the way come group actions and the art of counting orbits, the Sylow theorems, modules over principal ideal
domains, finite fields, and the quaternions that describe the rotations of space. Every result is proved in full, with
graded exercises, complete solutions that explain how to find the idea, and a Python notebook per chapter where groups
are built and counted, and algebra meets error-correcting codes and cryptography.
