\chapter{Moments of \texorpdfstring{$L$}{L}-functions}\label{ch:oc-moments}\label{ch:moments}

\begin{center}\itshape
How large is $|\zeta|$ on the rare occasions when it is large, and why should the answer involve the number $42$?
\end{center}

This chapter is about the moments of $\zeta$ on the critical line. It proves the mean square, with its secondary term; lower bounds of the conjectured order for every integer moment; and the arithmetic factor $a_k$, read from the random Euler product of Chapter~\ref{ch:oc-values}. The matrix factor is a theorem of Volume~12. Past the fourth moment, the asymptotic of $\zeta$ itself is a conjecture.

Two tools come from Chapter~\ref{ch:oc-values}, which is placed after this one because it is longer, but which does not use anything from this chapter: a mean value theorem for Dirichlet polynomials and a twisted second moment. A third tool, the approximate functional equation, is \emph{not} proved in this book, and it is used only in heuristics, each marked as such: the recipe that writes $\zeta(\frac12+it)^2$ as a sum of length $t/2\pi$ plus a dual sum of the same length, and Exercise~\ref{ex:diagonal4}. The proved results do not use it. The factorisation of $\zeta$ by Gonek, Hughes and Keating is quoted as their theorem, and the independence of its two factors is a conjecture.
