The Erd\H os--Kac theorem is already a theorem of Volume~6 (Theorem~9.7). Here it falls out of the Kubilius model in a few lines, and the two proofs show different things. The Sathe--Selberg formula, which sharpens the count of integers with $\omega(n)=k$, is Cauchy's integral of the Selberg--Delange mean already proved in this chapter; it is not rewritten here.

\begin{theorem}[Erd\H os--Kac, as a corollary]\label{thm:erdoskac-cor}
For every real $a$, the proportion of $n\le x$ with $(\omega(n)-\log\log x)/\sqrt{\log\log x}\le a$ tends to $\Phi(a)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $L=\log\log x$ and $y=x^{1/\log L}$, so that $u=\frac{\log x}{\log y}=\log L\to\infty$ and $y\le x^{1/4}$ for $x$ large.

\emph{The large primes are negligible.} An integer $n\le x$ has at most $u$ prime factors larger than $y$, so $0\le\omega(n)-\omega_y(n)\le\log L$, where $\omega_y(n)=\sum_{p\le y}\mathbf 1_{p\mid n}$. Since $\log L=o(\sqrt L)$, it is enough to prove the theorem for $\omega_y$.

\emph{The model.} The function $\omega_y(n)$ is a function of the vector $v_y(n)$ of exponents of the primes $p\le y$, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:kubilius} its law under $P_x$ is within $C_2(L^{-1/4}+x^{-1/4}\log x)=o(1)$ in total variation of the law of $W=\sum_{p\le y}B_p$, where the $B_p=\mathbf 1_{G_p\ge1}$ are independent Bernoulli variables of mean $\frac1p$. By Mertens (Volume~6, Theorem~1.5), $\mathbb EW=\sum_{p\le y}\frac1p=\log\log y+O(1)=L-\log L+O(1)$, and $s^2=\operatorname{Var}W=\sum_{p\le y}\frac1p(1-\frac1p)=L+O(\log L)$.

\emph{The central limit theorem in the model.} The variables $X_p=\frac{B_p-1/p}{s}$ are independent, centred, with $\sum_p\mathbb EX_p^2=1$ and $|X_p|\le\frac1s\to0$, so Lindeberg's condition holds trivially and Lindeberg's theorem (Volume~4, Theorem~6.10) gives $\mathbb Ef(\frac{W-\mathbb EW}s)\to\mathbb Ef(Z)$ for every $f$ with three bounded continuous derivatives. For real $a$ and $\delta>0$, squeeze $\mathbf 1_{]-\infty,a]}$ between two such functions that agree with it outside $[a-\delta,a+\delta]$; since $\Phi$ is continuous, $P(\frac{W-\mathbb EW}s\le a)\to\Phi(a)$.

\emph{Back to the integers.} Total variation $o(1)$ moves each probability by $o(1)$, so $P_x(\frac{\omega_y(n)-\mathbb EW}s\le a)\to\Phi(a)$ for every $a$. Finally $\frac{\mathbb EW-L}{\sqrt L}\to0$, $\frac s{\sqrt L}\to1$ and $\frac{\omega-\omega_y}{\sqrt L}\to0$ uniformly; a shift and a rescaling that tend to $0$ and $1$ do not change the limit of a distribution function at a point of continuity of $\Phi$, which is every point.
\end{proof}

Volume~6 proves the same limit by the method of moments, truncating at $z=x^{1/L^{1/4}}$ and comparing the moments of $\omega_z$ with those of the independent sum, then smoothing. The two arguments use the same truncation. They do not use the same bookkeeping.

What Volume~6 makes visible is the moment comparison: after the large prime factors are cut away, $\omega$ has the moments of a sum of independent coin tosses, and those moments are the Gaussian moments. The proof never builds a probability space on the primes; the independence is an asymptotic statement about averages of $\mathbf 1_{p\mid n}$.

What this chapter makes visible is the space. Theorem~\ref{thm:kubilius} is a total-variation comparison, so the coin tosses are a measure, not only a list of moments. The bell curve is the central limit theorem on that product, which Volume~4 already contains. The arithmetic content is the estimate that says the product is close to the integers.

The rate in the corollary is not the one R\'enyi and Tur\'an obtained from the Selberg--Delange formula. That rate is $O(1/\sqrt{\log\log x})$ in the Kolmogorov distance, and it uses the mean of $z^{\omega(n)}$ for complex $z$ of modulus $1$, which this chapter has proved and which Volume~6 does not need. Recording the rate would be a second pass through the same generating function, not a second foundation. Volume~11's description of a uniform integer modulo $\varphi$, which quotes the Selberg--Delange theorem from this chapter, is a consumer of the mean-value theorem, not of a second proof of Erd\H os--Kac.

\begin{proposition}[a Chernoff bound]\label{prop:chernoffomega}
Let $Q^*(\alpha)=\alpha\log\alpha-\alpha+1$ for $\alpha>0$, and fix $A\ge1$. Uniformly for $x\ge16$,
\[
P_x\bigl(\omega(n)\ge\alpha\log\log x\bigr)\ll_A(\log x)^{-Q^*(\alpha)}\qquad(1\le\alpha\le A).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Selberg's formula in this chapter gives $\mathbb E_x z^{\omega(n)}\ll_A(\log x)^{z-1}$ for real $z\in[1,A]$. Markov's inequality at $z=\alpha$ produces the exponent $\alpha-1-\alpha\log\alpha=-Q^*(\alpha)$.
\end{proof}
