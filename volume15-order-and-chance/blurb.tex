Can pure order and pure chance be told apart? The primes are defined without probability, and yet they, the M\"obius function and the class groups of quadratic fields all behave, in part, like random objects.
This last volume of the series asks, statistic by statistic, when that resemblance is a theorem and when it is only a model.\par\medskip
It proves Maier's theorem, that the primes break Cram\'er's random model in short intervals, and Selberg's theorem, that $\log|\zeta(\frac12+it)|$ is Gaussian at a random height. Between them come the Kubilius model and the Erd\H os--Kac theorem, sieves read as probability, random multiplicative functions, random groups for class groups and Selmer groups, the zeros of zeta against random matrices, moments of $\zeta$, pseudorandomness and the prime race.\par\medskip
Every result is proved, or quoted with its source, or labelled as a conjecture. The book ends with a ledger of what the series proved and two open projects.
