## V4-10 — Uniqueness and inversion

`volume4-chance/chapters/ch06-clt.tex`, from the continuity display after the Gaussian calculus through the end of part (b) of Theorem (uniqueness and inversion). The statement is unchanged, including for a law with no mean.

The proof no longer quotes the Chapter 5 identity `E[R_m-L_m]=2^{-m}(Q(1)-Q(0))`. That identity needs finite endpoint values. A Cauchy quantile has none, and `E|Y-L_m|` need not be small.

What replaces it is the grid-sum definition in Proposition (test functions). A finitely atomic law `X_*` is read off a continuity grid of `F`. For that law the smoothed density is a finite mixture of normals, and a finite sum puts the Gaussian formula inside the integral. The grid error is uniform on bounded frequency intervals because `e^{itx}` is Lipschitz there, and the tails of `F` are small without a moment: a tail mass `η` moves a characteristic function by at most `2η`. The same grid, with a Lipschitz constant that does not depend on the centre, identifies `E[ψ_σ(y-X)]` with the damped Fourier integral of `φ_X`.

Continuity of `φ_X` is proved before it is used, by `|φ(t+h)-φ(t)|≤2P(|X|>A)+|h|A`. The cutoff `A` is not `E|X|`. Dominated convergence in part (b) then applies to a continuous integrand. The integral of the inverted density over `[-R,R]` uses a radius with `P(|X|=R)=0`; arbitrarily large such radii exist because `|X|` has countably many atoms. Two bounded uniformly continuous cutoffs sandwich the smoothed tail, and at that continuity point `P(|X+σZ|>R)` tends to `P(|X|>R)`. Exercise 6.4 still derives `e^{-|t|}` from the damped Cauchy integral and does not use this reduction. The paragraph after the theorem still feeds the integrable transform `1/(1+t^2)` of the two-sided exponential into part (b); that integration by parts is unchanged. Later volumes cite the statement of Theorem 6.8, which was not weakened, so their citations were left as they stand.
