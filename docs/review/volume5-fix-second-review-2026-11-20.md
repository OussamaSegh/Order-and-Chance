## Verdicts

**Correctness.** The reviewed head needed changes. Five defects are listed below. After those corrections, each of V5-001 through V5-021 and V5-023 is fixed in the place the issue named, and the fix does the job the issue asked for. V5-022 is a line-number gutter and is rightly left unchanged. Nothing extra was rewritten.

**Dropped or weakened proofs.** No numbered proof was shortened. The sine product, the strict tail, the connected-graph limit, the cotangent identification, and the Gibbs argument keep the same conclusions. The corrections add the missing steps.

**Theorem 6.17, both directions.** On the reviewed head the identity \(L^2-4\pi A=4\pi^2\sum(n^2-n)|c_n|^2\) was already an equality, and \(n^2-n=0\) only for \(n\in\{0,1\}\). The sentence that followed said this was the curve \(z(s)=c_0+c_1e^{is}\). Vanishing coefficients give that formula only through Dirichlet's theorem, which was not cited, and the formula was not shown to force the coefficients to vanish. The corrected proof does both. If the other coefficients vanish, Theorem `thm:dirichletfourier` says the partial sums converge to \(z\), and for \(N\ge1\) the partial sum is the trigonometric polynomial. If \(z(s)=c_0+c_1e^{is}\), orthonormality of the \(e_n\) gives those coefficients and no others, so every term in the sum is zero. For \(c_1=re^{i\varphi}\) with \(r>0\), the point stays at distance \(r\) from \(c_0\), the argument increases by \(2\pi\), and \(|z'|=r\), so the radius is \(|c_1|\) and the length is \(2\pi r\). The constant curve has \(L=0\) and \(A=0\). A clockwise circle is the mode \(n=-1\), and a double traversal is the mode \(n=2\); both leave a positive term in the sum.

**Corollary 13.4.** The integral identity \(\varpi/\pi=1/M(1,\sqrt2)\) is the same substitution as before. The length \(2\varpi\) is now derived. Each half of the right-hand loop has length \(\int_0^1(1-r^4)^{-1/2}\,\mathrm dr\), the right-hand loop has length \(\varpi\), and the half-turn \((x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)\) carries that loop onto the left-hand loop and preserves speed. The reviewed proof cited Chapter 6 as the definition of length. Chapter 6 only sets the speed of a curve equal to \(L/(2\pi)\). The correction defines the length as the integral of the speed, which returns \(L\) in that constant-speed case, and it writes \(|\mathrm dr|/\sqrt{1-r^4}\) for both halves of the loop.

**`cor:cotagain`.** Corollary 8.12 had no label. The new label sits on that corollary, the index line points at it, and `number_volume(5)` still calls it corollary 8.12. No other label uses the name.
