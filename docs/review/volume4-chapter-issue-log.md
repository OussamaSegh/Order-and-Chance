## Chapter 11 (martingales) stopping rules, not yet reviewed

The file is `ch10-martingales.tex`; it is the eleventh chapter. Optional stopping now has three hypotheses: a deterministic bound on the time, bounded steps with finite mean time, and a deterministic bound on the stopped variables together with finite mean time. The first-win time has infinite mean, because a finite mean would force the stopped fortune to be $0$. The fair walk from $1$ to the barriers $0$ and $3$ has mean duration $2$ and stopped mean $1$. The statement at `fd4a5e41` put $\mathbb E[\tau]<\infty$ into both extra hypotheses and could be read as saying those hypotheses were necessary. The revised statement lists three sufficient conditions and does not call any of them necessary. Condition (2) uses bounded steps and a finite mean: $\tau$ is almost surely finite because $P(\tau>n)\le\mathbb E[\tau]/n$, $X_\tau$ is integrable because $|X_\tau-X_0|\le c\tau$, and the expectations converge because the difference after time $n$ is at most $c(\tau-n)$, whose mean is the tail $\sum_{j>n}P(\tau\ge j)$. Condition (3) uses a uniform bound $|X_{\tau\wedge n}|\le M$ and almost-sure finiteness. Then $|X_\tau|\le M$, so integrability does not need a finite mean, and the expectations converge because the difference is at most $2M\,P(\tau>n)$.
