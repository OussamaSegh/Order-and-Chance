## Proof-bearing assertions, individually checked

| Claim | Mathematical check and dependency |
| --- | --- |
| `prop:bmgauss` | For an ordered time grid, differences of the centred jointly Gaussian endpoint vector form a jointly Gaussian increment vector; covariance `min` gives diagonal increments with variances `t_i-t_(i-1)`. Conversely an ordered vector of independent centred normal increments maps by partial sums to a joint Gaussian vector and gives `Cov(B_s,B_t)=s`. Disjoint nonconsecutive increments are grouped on a common sorted endpoint grid. The drift counterexample really refutes the weaker, uncentred formulation. |
| `lem:levylevel` | For each parent increment `D ~ N(0,2^(-n+1))` and independent midpoint normal, children `D/2 ± 2^(-(n+1)/2)Z` have both variance `2^(-n)`, zero mutual covariance and joint Gaussianity. Across parents both prior increments and fresh normals are independent. Correct indexing and base case. |
| `thm:levy` | Midpoint Gaussian displacements at level `n` have variance `2^(-(n+1))`. At threshold `sqrt(2n) 2^(-n/2)`, `2^n` tails yield a summable `O(2^n e^(-2n))` union bound. The resulting sup-norm refinements are summable, giving continuous uniform limit. Approximating finitely many interval endpoints by dyadics, and applying the explicit small-oscillation event, transfers centred Gaussian marginals and product joint laws to arbitrary disjoint intervals. Same-copy disjoint intervals use the Brownian increment property; different unit copies use distinct uniform streams. |
| `prop:scaling` | Linear time/space scaling preserves increment law and continuity. Inversion `t B_(1/t)` has joint Gaussian covariance `st min(1/s,1/t)=min(s,t)`. Continuity at zero follows from summable integer-time Gaussian deviation tails plus unit-interval oscillations bounded by the midpoint hierarchy, with explicitly summable outer-content tails. For an arbitrary Brownian process the Gaussian midpoint innovations have the same finite-dimensional laws as in the construction, by the preceding covariance characterization. |
| `lem:squares` | Integration by parts against `phi'=-x phi` gives fourth standard normal moment 3; normal scaling gives `E ξ^4=3σ^4`, hence `Var(ξ²)=2σ^4`. Gaussian decay kills the boundary term. |
| `thm:qv` | The full partition, including any short endpiece, has mean `t` and variance `2 Σ h_j² ≤ 2t 2^(-n)`, proving `L²` convergence. Almost-sure proof **after #134** uses the finite-coordinate dyadic prefix `V_n` with `Var V_n=2t_n2^(-n)`; outer-content Chebyshev and summable bounds give `V_n−t_n→0` for each fixed `t`. Uniform path continuity separately kills `(B_t−B_(t_n))²`. The original illegitimate finite-coordinate argument at non-dyadic endpoints is no longer used. |
| Unlabeled `corollaryx` (variation) | A continuous finite-variation path has `Σ (ΔB)² ≤ max|ΔB| · TV(B)`; mesh uniform continuity gives `max|ΔB|→0`, contradicting the dyadic quadratic variation limit 1. This is an extra audited assertion not represented by a theorem `\label` or an exercise ID. |
| `thm:dyadicito` | Taylor's integral remainder is bounded by `(1/2) ω(max|ΔB|) Σ(ΔB)²→0` pathwise. Split the quadratic sum into `h Σ f''(B_prev)` and centred `Σ Z_k`. The first converges by Riemann integrability along continuous paths. For `j<k`, future increments are independent of all variables up to `k−1` and `E(Δ_k²−h)=0`, making cross terms zero; `E(1/2 ΣZ_k)²≤||f''||∞²/(2N)`. The text correctly does **not** claim almost-sure convergence of that centred term. |
| `thm:pwz` | For three consecutive length-`h` increments, independent Gaussian small-ball probabilities `O(K√h)^3`, summed over at most `h^(-1)` positions, are `O(K³√h)`, summable over dyadic levels. A differentiable point would force three nearby increments to have size at most `6Kh<8Kh`, violating eventual absence of such blocks. Integer `K` countability handles every finite derivative at every interior `t`. |
| `prop:bmmart` | At sufficiently fine dyadic ceiling times, early observations are functions of increments before `s_n`, independent of the future centred increment, so product expectation is zero. Brownian increment `L²` continuity and bounded continuous `g`, with uniform continuity on a high-probability compact cube, justify the limit to general real times. No unconstructed full-filtration conditional expectation is asserted. |
| `prop:restart` | The hit event by deterministic `s` is determined by the path up to `s` using finite-grid minima and continuity. Approximate the first hit after `q` by the grid-valued ceiling `τ_n`; on each event `{τ_n=s}`, the post-`s` finite increment vector is independent of the stopped past. Sum over finitely many grid values up to `R`, then use path continuity and bounded test-function limits, and finally `R→∞`. The `τ=R` boundary has probability zero for `R>q`, due to normal density. This proves the hitting-time restart used later, not an unrestricted strong Markov theorem for arbitrary stopping times. |
| `thm:reflection` | Hit `a` iff maximum exceeds `a`; restart at that first hit and reflect future centred Brownian increments. Since endpoint above `a` necessarily hit the barrier, `P(M_t≥a)=2P(B_t≥a)`. Differentiating `2(1−Φ(a/√t))` yields `a/(√(2π)t^(3/2)) exp(−a²/(2t))`. Its `t^(−3/2)` tail implies infinite mean by comparison. |
| `prop:bmsquare` | Expand `B_t²−B_s²=2 B_s ΔB+(ΔB)²`; future increment is independent of the joint past observation vector, with mean zero and second moment `t−s`. Bounded `g` and Cauchy–Schwarz make the mixed factor integrable. This yields the finite-observation martingale identity without falsely applying the bounded-`g` proposition to unbounded `B_s g`. |

## All seven complete exercise triples

| Exercise | Statement/hint/solution validation |
| --- | --- |
| `ex:maxjoint` | Reflection after first hit `a` maps endpoint `x≤b≤a` to `2a−x≥2a−b≥a`; the reflected event's hit condition is automatic from its endpoint. |
| `ex:bridge` | Expand four covariances to obtain `min(s,t)−st`; centred jointly Gaussian bridge coordinates are orthogonal to `B_1`, hence independent as a vector. Dense dyadic approximation passes bounded continuous test identities to every real `s,t∈[0,1]`. The explicitly noted `t=2` counterexample has `Var(B_2−2B_1)=2` and invalid extrapolation `2−4=−2`. |
| `ex:zeros` | Gaussian left-endpoint density bound and reflection oscillation estimate give total dyadic covering length `L_n` with summable `E L_n`; outer-length zero follows. Restart at first zero after each rational `q` gives immediate positive and negative oscillations and rules out any isolated zero before 1. `P(B_1=0)=0`; a binary nested-interval construction inside the closed nonempty perfect zero set yields an uncountable injection. |
| `ex:hittingdensity` | Reflection/scaling give `P(T_a≤t)=2[1−Φ(a/√t)]=P(a²/B_1²≤t)`; `B_1=0` is probability zero. |
| `ex:pvariation` | `V_n=Σ|ΔB|²→1`, `m_n=max|ΔB|→0`. Bounds `Σ|ΔB|^p≤m_n^(p−2)V_n` for `p>2` and reverse for `p<2` prove all three pathwise limits (eventually `m_n>0` since `V_n>0`). |
| `ex:putcall` | Pathwise `(S_T−K)^+−(K−S_T)^+=S_T−K`; compare a long call/short put against a stock position financed by lending/borrowing discounted strike. Mispricing admits long-cheap/short-expensive replication with identical terminal payoff. |
| `ex:onestep` | Mean zero and covariance `Cov(B_t,B_t)=t` imply `E B_t²=t`. |
