# Volume 1 — First Principles

**Score: 7/10.**

The book does the job it announces. A reader with secondary-school algebra can follow induction derived from well-ordering, four proofs of the sum of cubes, inclusion–exclusion, the five theorems on a closed interval, a sequential proof that a nonconstant complex polynomial has a root, the spectral theorem for a real symmetric matrix, Stirling’s constant from the Wallis integrals, and Chebyshev’s weak law. The prose is a person teaching: short sentences, a number next to the claim, and almost none of the banned filler. The score stays at 7 because thirteen justifications that the text treats as finished are not. The leastness properties of cosine are used as if the preface had stated them. The Erdős–Ko–Rado count names the arcs that meet a fixed arc without classifying them. The exponent test for a least common multiple is used in the harmonic solution without the divisibility lemma. Carleman’s constant is called sharp by an unproved passage from termwise equivalence to partial sums. Complex multiplicity is differentiated with a real power rule. The Hilbert determinant is declared positive by an exercise the reader is told to postpone. The secant plate draws a vertically compressed parabola and labels the slopes of \(y=x^2\). No main theorem was found false.

**Counts.** 0 critical, 13 major, 78 minor. Ninety-one issues, `V1-001` through `V1-091`.

**How this was read.** Book order in `volume1-first-principles/main.tex`, with the chapter counter started at \(-1\): preface and jacket; Chapter 0 Language; 1 Sums; 2 Counting; 3 Sequences; 4 Orders; 5 Complex numbers; 6 Polynomials; 7 Linear algebra; 8 Calculus; 9 Inequalities; 10 Probability; field guide, image credits, index, About the Cover. Every chapter file was read, including exercises, hints, and solutions, and the plates those chapters input. Every major quotation below was re-opened in the source. A proof named under Verified correct was read line by line, or its displayed algebra was recomputed. Historical memoirs, the PDF build, and portrait files were not reopened; those items are under Not checked.

**Style, once.** The banned openers and intensifiers (`delve`, `it is important to note`, `in this section we will`, `in conclusion`, `robust`, `comprehensive`, `seamless`, `leverage`, `journey` as filler, `tapestry`, `unlock`, `pivotal`, `underscore`, `realm`) do not recur. The one `journey` is the car in the mean-value sentence (`ch05-sequences-series.tex:562`) and is a concrete trip. Two habits do recur, and the rows below are the instances: a true one-line reason is left unwritten, and a later or standing hypothesis is cited as if an earlier page had already stated it. No notebook is mentioned. Reader-facing copy does not call the book a revision.
