**Score: 7/10.**

The book does the job the preface announces. A reader who has Volumes 1 and 2 can follow the group axioms, Lagrange, the classification of cyclic groups, the isomorphism theorems, orbit–stabiliser, Burnside, the fifteen-puzzle, simplicity of \(A_n\) for \(n\ge 5\), the Sylow theorems, the failure of unique factorisation in \(\Z[\sqrt{-5}]\), Gauss’s lemma for \(\Z[X]\), the structure of finitely generated modules over a PID, the tower law, Wantzel’s obstruction, the field \(\mathbb F_q\), and the character table of \(\mathfrak S_4\). The prose is a person teaching: a square, a necklace, or a matrix next to the claim, and none of the banned filler. The score is 7 because four written arguments treat a step as finished when the page does not contain it.
