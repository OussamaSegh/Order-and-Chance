### Justifications

**Chapter 1.** The opener (p. 2) is the right kind of hook: Bernoulli's tenth-power sum is printed as `91 409 924 241 424 243 424 241 924 242 500`, and that is \(\sum_{k=1}^{1000} k^{10}\). The fundamental theorem, falling powers, and summation by parts are stated cleanly. Two notations are used before they are fixed. \(H_n\) is defined as \(1+\cdots+1/n\), and Example `ex:k2k` then sums \(H_k\) from \(k=0\) (`ch01-finite.tex:96`, `:150`). The identity is right if \(H_0=0\), and the book never says so. The same section writes the Kronecker symbol as \([n=0]\) (`:306`) with no definition in this volume or in Volume 1. The exercise solutions were sampled, not all re-solved. The staircase figure was read from its caption and source, not from a render.

**Chapter 2.** Franklin's proof of the pentagonal theorem (`thm:pentagonal`, `:336–379`) was read line by line. The involution is correct. The two exceptional families are declared disjoint (`:376`) without the one-line check; they are disjoint, because \(r(3r-1)=s(3s+1)\) forces \(3(r-s)=1\). The recurrence was used to recompute \(p(n)\) through \(n=1000\), including \(p(200)=3972999029388\). The Glaisher solution (`:1172–1174`, p. 50) ends with a false sentence: \(7+2+2+2+2+1+1+1\) does not have all multiplicities at most 2, which is the property of the image \(7+6+3+2\). The first half of the chapter (rational series, the exponential formula) and most of the nineteen closing problems were not re-solved.

**Chapter 3.** The product convention (`:31–37`) is explicit: a product converges when the partial products have a limit, and that limit may be 0. That is a stated convention, not a slip. Tonelli is labelled admitted and pointed at Volume 7, Theorem 9.5 (`:314`). The sentence that the irrationality of \(\zeta(5)\) is unknown (`:229`, p. 68) was the standard status through 2025. A formalized argument has been public since October 2026 (see V5-m7); the proof was not rechecked here. The cotangent expansion and the sine product were not re-derived line by line. Their later uses, in the reflection formula and in \(\zeta(2k)\), have the classical shape.

**Chapter 4.** Differentiation under the integral sign, the dominated-convergence corollary for continuous functions, and the parameter proof of \(\int_0^\infty e^{-x^2}\,dx=\sqrt\pi/2\) were read and are correct. Two sentences are not. The parameter proof is called the third proof of the Gaussian integral, the second being "Volume 4, Chapter 5, by polar coordinates" (`ch04-integrals.tex:145–146`, p. 82). Volume 4, Chapter 5, cites Volume 1, Chapter 8, for the value, and uses polar rectangles for Box–Muller. It does not evaluate the integral. The BBP proposition (`prop:digits`, p. 86) correctly says that the fractional part of \(16^d\pi\) gives hexadecimal digits from position \(d+1\). The next paragraph pairs \(d=10^6\) with `26C65E52`. Exact modular arithmetic gives `26C65E52` at \(d=999999\) and `6C65E52C` at \(d=10^6\). Bailey, Borwein, and Plouffe (Math. Comp., 1997) printed `26C65E52` as the digits from position \(10^6\); Bailey's later note of the algorithm states that \(d=10^6\) begins at position \(10^6+1\), with digits `6C65E52…`. The solution of `ex:swapfail` records the spike \(f_n(1/n)=n/e\) and then says only that domination fails. On \([0,1]\), which is a closed bounded subinterval of the domain, the convergence is not uniform either, so both hypotheses of `cor:dct` fail.

**Chapter 5.** The Beta evaluation of \(\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt\pi\), the reflection formula, and Legendre's duplication were read. The functional equations fed to Bohr–Mollerup check, and the sine product is cited for the limit. The double-integral proof is refused, with Tonelli named and deferred to Volume 7 (`:248–251`). That is the right discipline. Bohr–Mollerup's own proof, and the log-convexity lemma it rests on, were not re-read. The parenthetical "a third method" for the Gaussian integral (`:262`) does not match Chapter 4's count; the evaluation itself is right. \(\Gamma(1/4)=3.62560990822\), recomputed with mpmath, matches the digits used later with the AGM.

**Chapter 6.** Fejér's theorem and the uniqueness corollary that Poisson summation needs (`cor:fejer`(b)) were read; the tail bound is the standard one from \(F_N(\theta)\le 1/(N\sin^2(\theta/2))\). Weyl's criterion for \(n\alpha\) was read. Parseval's proof was only begun. The exercise `pb:siegel` admits the volume of a convex body (`:496–498`) and says "Volume 7 constructs this integral," without a theorem number. The preface (p. v) says every admission names the place of the proof. The Weyl figure (`:337–339`) states the three-gap theorem in the caption. It is neither proved nor listed in the chapter's sources.

**Chapter 7.** The opening history was checked against Heideman, Johnson, and Burrus, *IEEE ASSP Magazine*, 1984. Gauss's worked transform of length 12 is the orbit of Pallas. Juno's eccentricity is one of the clues used to date the manuscript, not the orbit that was interpolated. The sentence on p. 142 says Juno. The DFT, the FFT recurrence, and the exercise solutions were not re-derived line by line. No false transform identity was found in the sections that were opened.

**Chapter 8.** This chapter carries the tools Chapters 10 and 12 spend, and one of them is stated a little short of its proof. The Gaussian transform (`thm:gaussianft`) is correct: \(\int e^{-\pi x^2}\,dx=1\) follows from Chapter 4 by \(x=u/\sqrt\pi\), and the scaling \(e^{-\pi x^2/a^2}\mapsto a\,e^{-\pi a^2\xi^2}\) is the convention the eta proof uses. Poisson summation (`thm:poisson`) asks for the decay \((1+|x|)^{-2}\) on both \(f\) and \(\hat f\), unfolds the coefficients, and quotes Fejér uniqueness. That hypothesis is the one the eta proof invokes, and the Gaussians meet it. The theta relation is the case \(a=t^{-1/2}\). The Gauss-sum evaluation (`thm:gausssign`) was checked from the completion of the square through the four cases. Each case is right. The parenthetical \(i^{-n}=1,-i,-1,i\) is the order \(n\equiv 0,1,2,3\), while the cases are printed \(n\equiv 1,2,3,0\). The Fresnel contour that supplies \(\int e^{2\pi iy^2/n}\,dy=(1+i)\sqrt n/2\) was not re-read. The heat kernel is normalised correctly, and its transform is \(e^{-4\pi^2 t\xi^2}\).

The inversion package is not in the same state. Lemma `lem:approxid` (`:99–112`) says that if \(f\) is merely integrable, then the Gaussian convolution tends to \(f(x)\) at every \(x\). The proof chooses \(\delta\) from continuity at \(x\), and the claim is false at a jump. Theorem `thm:ftinversion` (`:114–120`) omits continuity from the box, uses the lemma, and concludes the inversion formula at every \(x\). The paragraph immediately after the box (`:122–128`) states the class that was actually proved: continuous, integrable, with integrable transform. Later uses in this volume stay in that class. The box and the lemma should say so. Heisenberg's inequality (`thm:heisenberg`) is the right bound on uncentred second moments, with equality for \(e^{-\pi x^2}\), which was checked. The next sentence (p. 178) says the inequality is about variances. Variance is the centred moment. Translating \(f\) increases \(\int x^2|f|^2\) and leaves \(|\hat f|\) unchanged, so the proved bound does not say what that sentence says.

**Chapter 9.** Cauchy's theorem on a star-shaped set, the integral formula, and the identity theorem are the tools Chapters 10 and 12 cite, and those citations match the statements. The residue computations that feed \(\zeta(2)\) were not all re-done. The chapter's map of what it leaves to Volume 8 (`:27–29`, p. 179) names the right subjects (homology form of Cauchy, Phragmén–Lindelöf, Hadamard factorisation, Mellin inversion) and the wrong chapter. In the current Volume 8 those subjects are Chapter 22, `ch09-complex.tex`. Chapter 14 is *Inequalities between Matrices*. The text says nothing in Volume 5 depends on them. That is true, and the pointer is still wrong.

**Chapter 10.** The functional equation was re-read. The Mellin identity, the substitution \(t=1/u\), the splitting \(\omega(1/u)=\sqrt u\,\omega(u)+\sqrt u/2-1/2\), the elementary integrals \(2/(s-1)\) and \(2/s\), and the residue 1 at \(s=1\) all check. So do \(\zeta(0)=-1/2\) and \(\zeta(1-2k)=-B_{2k}/(2k)\), including \(-1/12\), \(1/120\), \(-1/252\), \(1/240\). The smoothed sums \(\sum e^{-n/N}=N-1/2+O(1/N)\) and \(\sum ne^{-n/N}=N^2-1/12+O(1/N^2)\) were recomputed from the series for \(1/(e^h-1)\) and for \(\sinh\). The book says the general smooth cutoff is not proved, and does not use it. Zeros are marked as computations, with Odlyzko, Gourdon, and Platt–Trudgian named. The first ordinate 14.13 matches \(14.1347\ldots\) rounded to two decimals; the other nine ordinates were not recomputed. \(\zeta(1/2)\approx-1.4604\) matches \(-1.46035\). Exercise `ex:eta` asks for \(\eta(s)=(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s)\) on all of \(\operatorname{Re}s>0\), which includes the pole. The solution removes \(s=1\) and is otherwise right. The letter \(\xi\) is fixed for the completed zeta, and \(\Lambda\) is reserved for von Mangoldt (`:178–180`).

**Chapter 11.** The Euler–Maclaurin induction, including the sign of \(B_1=-1/2\), was checked. The harmonic expansion's constant term and the comparison of consecutive remainders were followed. At \(n=10\), the printed truncation through \(1/(252 n^6)\) errs by \(4.09\times 10^{-11}\), against the book's \(4.1\times 10^{-11}\), and the next term \(1/(240 n^8)\) is \(4.17\times 10^{-11}\), against \(4.2\times 10^{-11}\). Laplace's method and the transfer theorem were not re-read line by line. \(\gamma\) is correctly referred to Volume 1, Chapter 3.

**Chapter 12.** This is the chapter the plan asked to be rebuilt, and the rebuild holds. Theorem `thm:eta` was read line by line. On \(\tau=it\) the Gaussian \(e^{\pi ix}e^{-3\pi t(x-1/6)^2}\) has the right transform, the odd integers produce \(\cos(\pi n/6)\), and Poisson summation gives \(\eta(i/t)=\sqrt t\,\eta(it)\). The principal square root is holomorphic on the upper half-plane because \(\operatorname{Re}(-i\tau)=\operatorname{Im}\tau>0\). The two sides of the transformation are holomorphic and agree on \(\{it:t>0\}\), so the identity theorem extends them. At \(\tau=0.3+0.7i\), forty terms of the series differ by about \(10^{-51}\). The book says more than thirty places. The proof does not cite Volume 6.

The Hardy–Ramanujan argument was also read line by line. The constant \(1/(4\sqrt 3\,N')\) is \(\sqrt{12}\,y^2\) with \(y^2=1/(24N')\). The minor-arc bound is for the whole of \(y\le|x|\le 1/2\), not only near \(q=1\): it uses \(|\sin\pi x|\ge 2|x|\) and keeps only the \(m=1\) term of \(\log P(r)-\log|P(z)|\). The numerical factors \(0.879\) and \(1/(80y)\) are real. The passage from \(N'=n-1/24\) to \(n\) costs \(O(n^{-1/2})\), which the \(O(n^{-1/4})\) of the major arc absorbs. Recomputed ratios \(p(n)/\mathrm{HR}(n)\): \(0.873\) at \(n=10\), \(0.956\) at \(100\), \(0.969\) at \(200\), \(0.986\) at \(1000\), with \(p(1000)=24061467864032622473692149727991\). Rademacher's series is in a theorem box marked "quoted," and the text says it is not proved and that nothing depends on it. The modular transformation for a general matrix in \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)\) is stated without proof in the same section. The figure on p. 268 labels the dashed curve \(1-0.4432/\sqrt n\); the caption says \(0.4433\). With \(C=\pi\sqrt{2/3}\), one has \(1/C+C/48=0.44328798\ldots\), so the caption rounds correctly and the curve does not. The proof is correct and hard to read. Stages 3 and 4 are a thicket of Gaussian tails. The opening two pages are among the best prose in the book.

**Chapter 13.** Gauss's theorem via Newman's substitution was checked: \(t=\frac12(x-ab/x)\) does turn \(I(a_1,b_1)\) into \(I(a,b)\), and the squeeze by \(a_n\) and \(b_n\) is legitimate once \(a_1\ge b_1\). The lemniscate constant \(\varpi=\pi/M(1,\sqrt 2)=2.62205755429211981\ldots\) matches the printed digits. The ellipse of semi-axes \(2\) and \(1\) has perimeter \(9.688448220547676\ldots\). Legendre's relation was followed through the claim that \(W'\) vanishes and the limit \(k\to 0\); the derivative algebra was not expanded term by term. Brent–Salamin was recomputed at 800 decimal places. \(\pi_1=3.140579\ldots\). The absolute errors of \(\pi_2,\ldots,\pi_8\) have decimal logs \(-8.13,-18.74,-40.26,-83.62,-170.64,-344.98,-693.95\), all under the printed ceilings \(10^{-8}\) through \(10^{-693}\). These are numerical checks of the printed errors, not a second proof of Legendre's relation.

The chapter body ends at `ch12-agm.tex:329`. The exercises and the old problem set then run past line 2100. That warehouse is why pedagogy and exercises are the low scores. Mertens's theorem is admitted inside `pb:dickmanint`, and the body of the chapter does not use it. There is no "Problem Book" reference. The solutions that were fully checked are the AGM exercises and the numerical claims attached to them. The bulk of the `pb:` solutions were read only as statements, or not at all. They are listed under "Not reviewed."

## 3. Overall

**Score: 7.**

**Verdict.** The volume does the job its title announces. The pentagonal theorem, the transformation of \(\eta\), the Hardy–Ramanujan leading term, the functional equation, the values of \(\zeta\) at non-positive integers, Euler–Maclaurin, and Gauss's AGM were checked and hold, including the constant in \(p(n)\) and the branch of \(\sqrt{-i\tau}\). The prose, at its best, sounds like a person who has computed the thing and wants to show you. I would not publish the series as it stands. Volume 8's proof that a holomorphic function has a primitive cites the wrong theorem of this volume, and a large family of later citations still use the chapter numbers from before the rewrite. Inside the volume, the inversion theorem is boxed without the continuity its proof uses, and the BBP section prints the famous digit string at the wrong index. Those are repairs, not a rewrite. After them, and after an index, I would recommend the book.

## 5. Verified correct

These were checked in the source, not taken from a summary. A recomputed number is recorded as a numerical check.

- **Pentagonal theorem, Franklin's involution** (`thm:pentagonal`). The moves \(A\) and \(B\) are inverse on the non-exceptional partitions, and the exceptional sizes are the two pentagonal families. The disjointness line is missing (V5-m5); the families are disjoint.
- **Euler's recurrence and the partition table.** The recurrence produces \(p(4)=5\), \(p(10)=42\), \(p(100)=190569292\), \(p(200)=3972999029388\), \(p(1000)=24061467864032622473692149727991\). Numerical check of the recurrence, which the pentagonal theorem justifies.
- **Bernoulli's tenth-power sum**, p. 2. \(\sum_{k=1}^{1000}k^{10}=91409924241424243424241924242500\).
- **Gaussian integral by a parameter** (`ex:gaussfeynman`), apart from the sentence about Volume 4.
- **\(\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt\pi\)** from the Beta integral, and the statements of reflection and duplication. The duplication formula matches Legendre's form \(\Gamma(x)\Gamma(x+1/2)=2^{1-2x}\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(2x)\).
- **Fejér's theorem** and the uniqueness of continuous functions with vanishing Fourier coefficients, in the form Poisson summation uses.
- **Gaussian transform, Poisson summation, theta relation**, under the hypotheses as boxed for Poisson and as repaired in prose for inversion. \(\vartheta(0.01)=10\,\vartheta(100)\) leaves an error \(20e^{-100\pi}\approx 7.3\times 10^{-136}\). The book's "135 decimal places" is the right order. Numerical check.
- **Gauss sum, the four cases**, given the Fresnel value the lemma quotes. The case list is correct (V5-m13).
- **Transformation of \(\eta\)** (`thm:eta`), including the branch of the square root and the identity-theorem extension. Numerical check at \(\tau=0.3+0.7i\): forty terms differ by about \(10^{-51}\).
- **Lemma `lem:ptrans`**, both the general form and the form at \(\tau=it/(2\pi)\).
- **Hardy–Ramanujan leading term** (`thm:hr`), including the constant \(1/(4\sqrt 3\,n)\) and the minor-arc bound on the whole complementary arc. The ratios in the table match the recomputation. Numerical check of the ratios; the \(O(n^{-1/4})\) is a proved bound, not a fitted error.
- **Rademacher's formula is marked quoted**, and the text says nothing depends on it. The formula was not re-proved, and was not claimed to have been.
- **Functional equation** (`thm:functional`), the continuation, the trivial zeros, and \(\zeta(1-2k)=-B_{2k}/(2k)\). The residue at \(s=1\) is 1. \(\zeta(1/2)\approx-1.46035\).
- **Smoothed sums** for \(\sum n^0\) and \(\sum n\) with an exponential cutoff (`prop:smoothed`). The book declines the general cutoff and does not use it.
- **Euler–Maclaurin at the level of the induction**, and the harmonic expansion's printed errors at \(n=10\). Numerical check of those errors.
- **Gauss's AGM theorem** by Newman's substitution, the lemniscate constant, the perimeter \(9.688448220547676\ldots\) of the ellipse with semi-axes 2 and 1, and the Brent–Salamin error ceilings. The error ceilings are a numerical check.
- **Notation fixed in the chapters that use it.** Completed zeta is \(\xi\). Jacobi's theta is \(\vartheta\). The Fourier transform is \(\hat f(\xi)=\int f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}\,dx\).
- **Admissions that are marked.** Tonelli for non-negative integrands, pointed at Volume 7, Theorem 9.5, in Chapter 3 and in the Sondow solution. Mertens, admitted inside one closing problem and not used in a theorem. The general \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)\) transformation of \(\eta\), stated without proof.
- **No "Problem Book"** in Volume 5. No reader-facing "this is a revision." No promise of a companion notebook.
- **Production.** Print page size, part-page bleed to the physical corners, EPUB media check, and the sampled pages listed above.
