A child can state the open problems about whole numbers; their theorems call on the whole of mathematics. This book
studies the integers with elementary tools and finds at every turn that elementary does not mean simple. It goes from
divisibility, primes and congruences to the cyclic group of remainders modulo a prime and to quadratic reciprocity,
Gauss's golden theorem, proved more than once because each proof shows a different reason. Then it follows innocent
questions: which numbers are sums of two or four squares, how continued fractions solve Pell's equation, which
Diophantine equations can be solved at all. To answer them it enlarges the integers to Gaussian and Eisenstein
integers, to ideals, where unique factorisation is restored after it fails, and to the $p$-adic numbers. Every result
is proved in full. Each chapter tells the story behind its ideas, ends with graded exercises and complete solutions, and
comes with a Python notebook that follows the arithmetic into modern cryptography, from fast exponentiation to RSA.
