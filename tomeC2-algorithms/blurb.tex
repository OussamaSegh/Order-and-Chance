How do you sort a million numbers, route a parcel across a continent, or recognise a prime with a thousand digits,
and how do you know you cannot do much better? An algorithm is a proof that does something, and this book proves,
for each algorithm it presents, both that it is correct and how fast it runs.

Along the way: recurrences and amortised analysis, sorting networks, hashing and union--find, shortest paths and
strongly connected components, dynamic programming, flows and the blossom algorithm for matchings, linear programming
and duality, randomised algorithms from minimum cuts to streaming, fast multiplication of numbers, polynomials and
matrices, primality from Miller--Rabin to AKS, string searching and the Burrows--Wheeler transform, approximation and
online algorithms.

Every theorem is proved in full, every exercise has a hint and a reasoned solution, and every chapter comes with a
Python notebook that implements the algorithms and checks them against the bounds. Written for the pleasure of seeing
why things work.
