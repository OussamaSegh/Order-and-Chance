What is a real number, a limit, a length? The earlier volumes used the words. This book says what they are, and it proves each step.

It builds the real line from sets, and it shows that infinity comes in more than one size. A distance then frees the limit from coordinates: metric spaces, completeness, and Banach's fixed point. Topology keeps continuity after the distance is dropped. Compactness handles an infinite cover by finitely many pieces. Baire's theorem says that a typical continuous function has a derivative at no point. A closed curve that does not cross itself has an inside and an outside: that is the Jordan curve theorem.

The second part measures. Lebesgue's length, and a set that has none. An integral that slices values instead of the axis. Fubini, Tonelli, and the volume of the ball in every dimension. The spaces \(L^p\), complete. Densities as derivatives of measures. Weak convergence, and fractals whose dimension is not a whole number. A last chapter of problems asks what almost every real number looks like.

Every theorem is proved. Every exercise has a hint and a solution that shows how the argument was found.
