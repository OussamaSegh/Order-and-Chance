\chapter*{About the Cover}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{About the Cover}
\noindent A circle is drawn for every prime up to 53, with radius the square root of that prime. A coral dot marks integer coordinates whose squares add to the prime. Each prime one more than a multiple of four carries eight dots. The prime 2 has four dots, one for each sign in both coordinates. A prime three more than a multiple of four has no dot, and its circle is pale. The pale centre is the origin. Sums of Squares proves that a prime is a sum of two squares exactly in the dotted cases. In Gaussian and Eisenstein Integers the pale circles remain prime, while the coral dots split or ramify. The drawing was computed by the book's own TikZ cover generator.
