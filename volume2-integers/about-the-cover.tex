\chapter*{About the Cover}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{About the Cover}
\noindent The integers from 1 to the square of 71 are placed in a square spiral. Each prime inside the disc receives a coral dot. The pale central marker is 1, which is not prime. Stanislaw Ulam drew his prime spiral in 1963; Martin Gardner featured it in Scientific American in March 1964. Some diagonals carry many prime dots, while others carry few. The picture raises a question about those patterns rather than supplying a test for primality. It faces the chapter Primes, where divisibility, counting and proof turn a list of numbers into arithmetic questions. The drawing was computed by the book's own TikZ cover generator.
