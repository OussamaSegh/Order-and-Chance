The sum of the first ten cubes is 3025. So is the square of 55, and the integers from 1 to 10 add to 55. That check is not a proof for every positive integer.

Language and proof come first. Sums and induction, counting, sequences and limits, orders, complex numbers, polynomials, linear algebra, calculus, inequalities, a first probability, and a field guide follow. Counting asks how every guest can get the wrong hat. The orders chapter proves Dilworth's theorem and Sperner's theorem.

The proofs include the intermediate value theorem, and Stirling's formula with the constant. Then come the Cayley--Hamilton theorem, the spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices, and the weak law of large numbers.

Exercises run from one star to three, each with a hint and a solution that says where the idea came from before the proof is written down. Workshops stay on one question. One matches the powers of two five times, then gives 31 where 32 was expected. Another takes a medical test that raises mostly false alarms when the condition is rare.
