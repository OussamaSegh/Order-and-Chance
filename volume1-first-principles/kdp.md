# Description

The sum of the first ten cubes is 3025. So is the square of 55, and the integers from 1 to 10 add to 55. One check is not a proof. The sums chapter proves the identity for every positive integer, four ways: induction, a change of basis, Nicomachus's odd numbers, and L-shapes from a multiplication table.

Counting asks how every guest can get the wrong hat. The orders chapter proves Dilworth's theorem and Sperner's theorem. Roots of unity pick out every third binomial coefficient. A determinant measures how a linear map stretches area.

You will meet the intermediate value theorem and Stirling's formula, the Cayley–Hamilton theorem, the spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices, and the weak law of large numbers, each proved from the definitions in this volume.

Exercises run from one star to three. Each has a hint, and a solution that says where the idea came from before writing the proof. One workshop matches the powers of two five times, then gives 31. The matching power of two is 32. Another workshop looks at a medical test that raises mostly false alarms when the condition is rare. The field guide is a short list of questions for the next problem.
