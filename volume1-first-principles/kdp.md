# Description

A surprising pattern is easy to spot. Proving it is another matter.

The first ten cubes add to 3025—the square of 1 + 2 + ... + 10. A few examples suggest a rule; they cannot establish it. This book teaches you how to ask the right question, choose a useful definition and build a proof that someone else can check. It also asks a different question: if four guests receive their hats in random order, how likely is it that nobody gets their own? One question is about certainty; the other is about chance. Both can be answered with mathematics.

First Principles is the starting point of Order and Chance: The Hidden Architecture of Mathematics. The fifteen volumes treat proofs, arithmetic, symmetry, probability, calculation, analysis, geometry, randomness and number theory, and the meeting of structure with chance. Each volume builds on the previous foundations. Volume 2, The Integers, carries the methods developed here into primes, congruences and equations in whole numbers. This first volume stands on its own.

Starting with secondary-school algebra and elementary geometry—not prior courses in proof, calculus or abstract algebra—you will learn to read quantified statements, test conjectures, use induction, count without double-counting, and turn numerical patterns into arguments. You will encounter sequences and limits, order relations, groups and subgroups, complex numbers and roots of unity, polynomials, linear algebra, calculus, inequalities and finite probability. You will see how an identity gets several different proofs, why a sequence converges, how complex numbers describe turns, why matrices simplify problems with several unknowns, and how counting becomes probability. More substantial theorems are developed from their foundations rather than required as prior knowledge.

The chapters combine worked examples and proof-finding workshops with exercises graded from one to three stars. Every numbered exercise has a hint and a complete solution. Each solution separates how to find the idea from the final write-up. Optional advanced bridges are clearly signposted, so the main reading route remains accessible with a high-school foundation.

For curious readers, independent learners and anyone who wants to know not just that a formula works, but why.
