Date: 27 October 2026. Reviewed baseline: `d89152f3ca7a652d5554700ffe7509942ca9ef23`.
Book: **Mathematical Maturity**, first edition, approved **7 × 10 in** paperback.

## Scope and result

Read the preface, all eight chapters, all numbered exercises, hints and solutions,
the eight workshops, challenge statements and notes, captions, field guide and
image credits. Checked arguments, domains, zero cases, signs, numerical claims,
selected historical assertions and current open-problem status. Checked named
forward references against the actual manuscripts, including the local arithmetic
bridge, Volume 1's Lucas exercise, and Volume 4's Gamma chapters.

This review found substantive errors in the earlier manuscript. The corrections
below are implemented in the book sources and propagate to every reading format.
The earlier broad assurance in `ERRATA.md` that all Volume 0 mathematics had been
found correct is superseded by this review.

## Mathematical corrections

| Place | Finding | Implemented correction |
|---|---|---|
| Ch. 1, Faulhaber challenge | The proposed symmetry belongs to the sum polynomial, not the odd-power summand. The solution did not prove the requested general result. | Added a complete proof using forward differences, `S(-n-1)=S(n)`, and evenness after shifting by 1/2. |
| Ch. 1, polynomial-sum corollary | The asserted degree fails when its leading coefficient is zero. | Require a nonzero leading coefficient. |
| Ch. 1, Fermat solution | Borrowed a divisibility result already proved locally. | Cite this chapter's arithmetic bridge. |
| Ch. 2, equilateral-triangle solution | Claimed an incorrect dilation by 2. | The map `z ↦ 3g-z` is an isometry; the triangles are congruent. |
| Ch. 2, constructible polygons | Treated the five known Fermat primes as the complete set of Fermat primes. | State the theorem with arbitrary distinct Fermat primes and identify the five as the known ones. |
| Ch. 2, roots caption | Omitted the subgroup generated by 1. | Include the trivial group U₁. |
| Ch. 3, Mason–Stothers exercise and solution | Reversed one Wronskian sign. | `AB′-A′B = AC′-A′C = B′C-BC′`. |
| Ch. 3, secret sharing | Algebraic compatibility over the reals does not establish statistical secrecy; forcing exact degree 2 also changes the distribution. | Specify a finite field, independently uniform coefficients including zero, degree at most 2, and prove that every share pair has probability 1/p² for every secret. Distinguish this model from the real interpolation exercise. |
| Ch. 3, secret-sharing hint | Said each Lagrange weight has three factors. | Two factors for three nodes. |
| Ch. 3, Chebyshev closed form | Asymptotic claim fails for n=0; square-root sign in the cosh proof fails for negative t. | Restrict the asymptotic to fixed n≥1; prove the cosh identity for t≥0 and extend by evenness. |

| Ch. 3, Gauss–Lucas | Electrostatic interpretation fails at multiple roots, where the field is singular; the figure's hull is a quadrilateral, not a pentagon. | Qualify the interpretation away from charges and correct the caption. |
| Ch. 3, log-concavity | Called the displayed inequality Newton's sharp inequality. | Identify it as a consequence of the sharper Newton inequalities. |

| Ch. 4, integral Cauchy–Schwarz | Equality wording obscured zero functions and the interval hypothesis. | Require a<b and use linear dependence, including zero. |

| Ch. 5, condensation | Claimed monotonicity from the first term for every real logarithmic exponent. | Use eventual monotonicity for both condensations. |

Stronger bounds require their actual values. |
| Ch. 5, other edge cases | Alternating remainder may be zero; Kempner proof only showed ≤80; nth-root claim needs n≥1. | Use “between zero and the first omitted term”, exhibit a strict deficit in the first block, and state n≥1. |

## Historical and current-fact verification

New bibliographic entries were added where they support corrected prose. The
following links identify the evidence used, rather than relying on textbook lore.

- **Gauss classroom story:** distinguish later numerical/busywork embellishments
  from the documented person Bartels. [Brian Hayes, *Gauss's Day of Reckoning*](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/MATH417/Bibliografia/HayesGaussDayofReckoning.pdf).
- **Induction:** removed the categorical claim of first use in 1321; medieval
  Arabic examples precede Levi ben Gershon. [MacTutor, al-Karaji](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Al-Karaji/), with Rashed's historical study added to the chapter.
- **Faulhaber:** replaced the claim that his proof is “lost” by the narrower fact
  that the published formulas do not provide their derivation.
  [Knuth, *Johann Faulhaber and sums of powers*](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1993-61-203/S0025-5718-1993-1197512-7/S0025-5718-1993-1197512-7.pdf).
- **Wessel:** rediscovered in 1895; French translation in 1897.
  [MacTutor biography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Wessel/).
- **Descartes' rule:** proofs predate Gauss's 1828 proof.
  [Antonio Fontana, *A Proof of Descartes' Rule of Signs*](https://eiris.it/ojs/index.php/ratiomathematica/article/download/425/pdf).
- **Secret sharing:** the probabilistic claim requires the finite-field model.
  [Shamir's original MIT report](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/91c51bfc-5678-409c-80c5-44ce8fcdbadf).
- **Stirling:** lost Oxford scholarships after refusing the oaths, rather than
  being expelled. Removed an unsupported judgement about de Moivre's character.
  [MacTutor biography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Stirling/).
- **Euler:** distinguish writing, presentation and publication dates. The constant
  appears in work written in 1731, read in 1734 and published in 1740. Removed an
  unnecessary precise discovery date/age for the Basel result; its 1735 presentation
  remains. [Lagarias's historical survey](https://arxiv.org/html/1303.1856).
- **YBC 7289:** records a square-root approximation, not the algorithm used.
  [Fowler and Robson's study, described with the tablet](https://myslu.stlawu.edu/~dmel/mesomath/tablets/YBC7289).
- **Taylor and probability:** removed the claims that Taylor's result was ignored
  for fifty years and that the Pascal–Fermat letters were the first probability
  calculations. Removed an unverified attribution of the “sophomore's dream” nickname.
- **Fermat:** qualified the disputed birth date and added Barner's archival study.
  [MAA account with references](https://old.maa.org/press/periodicals/convergence/when-was-pierre-de-fermat-born?device=mobile).
- **Ballot problem:** distinguish the familiar reflection argument from André's
  original bijection; Renault's 2008 article is now cited in Ch. 8 as well as Ch. 1.
- **Hadamard frontier:** the old claim that order 668 remained unknown was outdated.
  The October 2026 survey verifies the twelve newly announced orders below 2000
  and gives 2396 as the smallest open order. Both challenge and notes now carry
  the date and source. [Egan et al., 19 October 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27175v1).
- **R(5,5):** retained **43≤R(5,5)≤46**, verified against the authors' 2026 paper;
  replaced the preprint-only bibliography with the journal reference.
  [Angeltveit–McKay](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jgt.70029).
  The general lower-bound discussion is explicitly diagonal; recent exponential
  improvements for unequal parameters do not contradict it.
  [Morris's 2026 survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05221v1).
