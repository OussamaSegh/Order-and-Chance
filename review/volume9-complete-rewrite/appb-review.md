## Mathematical corrections and prerequisites
- Ptolemy retains all four-point configurations. Adjacent coincidences give automatic equality; opposite coincidences require separate handling. For distinct points equality requires cyclic order on a generalized circle.
- Concentric circles and both nesting orders are included in the coaxal inversion argument. The inversion pole is chosen in the inner disk when an annulus is required.
- Steiner closure uses distinct, consistently advancing circles and their first return. Backtracking alone does not give the advertised chain. Pappus inversion fixes a circle as a set, not its centre as a point.
- Descartes uses signed curvature for an enclosing circle and curvature zero for a line. A concrete 1,1,4,12 configuration swaps its fourth circle for a common tangent line. The coefficient-vector proof verifies all Gram identities and proves the required positive norm locally, without Appendix E or an unintroduced signature theorem.
- Gasket dimension and zero area are explicitly quoted results; finite drawings are illustrations.
- Mobius triple normalization treats infinity algebraically. The identity is separate from the nonidentity fixed-point cases.
- Feuerbach includes a local nine-point-circle proof and distinguishes coincident circles in the equilateral incircle case. Excircle tangency uses an explicit positive trigonometric expression, avoiding an unproved location assertion.
- CORRECTION: the old hexlet claim must allow generalized spheres. A closing member can be a plane; six genuine finite spheres require that none of the transformed boundaries pass through the inverse pole. Consistent advancement and the distinct six members are specified.
- All original solutions are expanded, including geometric placement in the twin-circle problem. Four new solutions cover the Descartes line branch, disk automorphisms, spherical image charges and a quantitative Ptolemy defect. Harmonicity and flux in the charge problem are proved with radial differentiation and the already taught divergence theorem.
