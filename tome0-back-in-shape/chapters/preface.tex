\chapter*{Preface}
This volume is the first of the series \emph{Numbers, Structures and Chance}. It is written for one reader:
someone who went through MPSI and MP$^*$, then engineering school, and who comes back to mathematics after ten
years, purely for pleasure.

Nothing here is new to that reader, and that is the point. Each chapter recalls the essentials in a few pages,
then spends most of its space on exercises worth doing. Exercises are graded:
\textbf{$\star$} warm-up, \textbf{$\star\star$} oral-exam level, \textbf{$\star\star\star$} gems.
Every exercise has a complete solution at the end of its chapter. Try first; read after.

Each chapter has a companion Python notebook, where the identities are tested numerically and put to work.

\paragraph{Two rules.} Every theorem, proposition and lemma is proved, including the facts recalled from the MP course; the only things taken without proof are the foundations named as such (the axioms of $\N$ and $\R$, the existence of the integral of a continuous function, and a few facts of topology), and results quoted for culture are labelled as remarks with a pointer to where they are proved. Every solution begins with \emph{How to find it}, which explains what to notice and why the method is natural, before the \emph{Write-up}.
