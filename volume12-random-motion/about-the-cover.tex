\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{About the Cover}
\noindent A single planar Brownian path, begun at the origin and stopped at the first moment it leaves the unit circle. Robert Brown recorded the irregular motion of particles suspended in pollen in 1827. The mathematical object, a random continuous path, was constructed by Norbert Wiener in 1923. This volume opens on that construction: the chapter Gaussian Processes and the Birth of Brownian Motion, followed by the chapter on the fine structure of a path.
