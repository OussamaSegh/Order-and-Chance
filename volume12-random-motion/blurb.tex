A pollen grain jiggling in water, a stock price and the eigenvalues of a random matrix all move to the same music: Brownian motion. This book builds it from Gaussian processes, studies the fine structure of its
paths, and develops the It\^o calculus that it requires. It then puts the motion to work: the heat equation and harmonic
functions, Girsanov's change of measure, stochastic differential equations from Ornstein--Uhlenbeck to Black--Scholes and
Dyson, and L\'evy processes.
The last part is networks and random matrices: a random walk as an electric current, recurrence by resistance, Kesten's theorem, and the matrix-tree theorem; Wigner's semicircle law and the sine kernel of random unitary matrices; and the branching random walk that governs the largest values of a random characteristic polynomial. Every result is proved, with one
marked exception at the research frontier. Each chapter opens with the story behind its ideas, ends with exercises and
complete solutions.
