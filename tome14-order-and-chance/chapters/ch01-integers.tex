\chapter{Order and chance in the integers}\label{ch:oc-integers}

This chapter asks what it means for a deterministic set of integers to look random, and which meanings are strong enough to be useful.

\section{Deterministic sets that look random}

The primes, the squarefree integers and the values of $\mu$ are defined without probability, yet many of their statistics match those of a random model. The aim is to separate that appearance from a theorem.

\section{What random-like should mean}

A useful notion has to name the statistic it predicts: frequencies in arithmetic progressions, correlations of a multiplicative function, or the spacing of a sequence. Later chapters fix one such notion at a time.
