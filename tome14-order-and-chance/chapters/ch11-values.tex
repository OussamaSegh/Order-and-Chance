\chapter{Value distribution}\label{ch:oc-values}

This chapter states the classical limit theorems for $\log\zeta$: the Bohr--Jessen theorem to the right of the critical line, and Selberg's central limit theorem on the line.

\section{Bohr--Jessen}

Bohr and Jessen proved that $\log\zeta(\sigma+it)$, for fixed $\sigma>\frac12$ and $t$ uniform in $[0,T]$, has a limiting distribution as $T\to\infty$. The proof uses the Euler product and the independence of $\log p$.

\section{Selberg's central limit theorem}

Selberg's theorem says that if $t$ is uniform in $[T,2T]$, then
\[
\frac{\log|\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|}{\sqrt{\tfrac12\log\log T}}
\]
tends in distribution to the standard Gaussian. Radziwi{\l}{\l} and Soundararajan gave a short proof that avoids zero-density estimates. The one-point statement is the input to the multipoint reading in Part~V; the mollifier in that argument sees $|\zeta|$ and not its argument.
