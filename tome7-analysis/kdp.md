# Description

In finite dimensions, a bounded sequence has a convergent subsequence, every linear map is continuous, and every operator has an eigenvalue. In infinite dimensions all three fail, and the repairs built between 1900 and 1935 are among the most useful ideas in mathematics. This book proves them, and then uses them.

It opens with the four pillars of functional analysis: Hahn-Banach, which produces linear forms out of nothing; Banach-Steinhaus, which turns pointwise bounds into uniform ones and shows that some continuous function has a divergent Fourier series; the open mapping and closed graph theorems; and weak compactness, where bounded sequences recover their subsequences. Hilbert spaces follow, with orthogonal projections, bases, and the spectral theorem for compact, then bounded, then unbounded self-adjoint operators, the last of which is the mathematical home of quantum mechanics.

Then Fourier analysis, in every setting at once: on the circle and on the line, with the inversion theorem, Plancherel's identity and the Poisson summation formula; on distributions, where differentiation is always possible and the Dirac delta is a function like any other; on finite groups, where it becomes the fast Fourier transform and the analysis of algorithms; and on locally compact groups, with Haar measure, Pontryagin duality, amenability and the p-adic numbers, which are self-dual.

The last three chapters are complex analysis in earnest. The Jordan curve theorem, proved in full from the Brouwer fixed point theorem. Cauchy's theorem in its final form, in Dixon's one-page proof. The Phragmen-Lindelof principle and the growth of the zeta function. Hadamard's factorisation of entire functions. The Riemann mapping theorem. The Mellin transform, which measures the cost of algorithms and reveals the microscopic oscillations hidden in them. And finally Tauberian theorems, from Tauber's own to Wiener's and Wiener-Ikehara, which recover a sequence from its transform and deliver the prime number theorem with no contour integral in sight.

Every theorem is proved in full. Every chapter opens with the story and the people behind the idea, and ends with exercises chosen for their beauty, each with a hint and a solution that explains how the argument is found. Every chapter comes with a Python notebook in which the ideas are put to work.

Written for the reader who wants to understand, not to pass an examination.
