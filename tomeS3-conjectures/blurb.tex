Every even number seems to be a sum of two primes, twin primes seem never to stop, and every starting number
seems to fall to 1 under the 3n+1 rule. Computers have checked these statements far beyond any doubt, and nobody
can prove them. This book takes twelve famous open problems and tells each one's whole story: who asked it,
what the evidence says, why we believe it, what is proved, and why the problem is still hard.

The journey runs through twin primes, Goldbach, the Riemann hypothesis and the random matrices hidden in the
zeros of zeta, the Collatz problem, Hadamard matrices, points and distances in the plane, the abc conjecture,
Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer, the randomness of the integers, and the inverse Galois problem. Every theorem is
proved, every record carries its date, and a Python notebook for each chapter lets you run the experiments.
