Chance seems the opposite of mathematics, yet it obeys laws as precise as any. This book starts with puzzles that a few
lines of counting settle and paradoxes where intuition fails, and climbs to the theorems that make randomness
predictable: the law of large numbers, the central limit theorem, the convergence of Markov chains and of martingales,
and Brownian motion, the continuous curve a random walk becomes when seen from far away. It ends with the probabilistic
method, where chance proves that objects exist without ever building them. Generating functions, entropy and Poisson
processes appear along the way. Every theorem is proved, apart from a few foundations of measure theory that are
clearly labelled and proved in a later volume. Each chapter has graded exercises with complete
solutions that explain how to find the idea.
