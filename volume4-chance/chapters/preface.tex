\chapter*{Preface}
A probability is a model. You name the outcomes, you name the weights, and only then do you compute. Volume~1 already did this for a finite experiment, and for one waiting time whose masses form a convergent series. It left a single question on the table: one sample space that holds every toss in an infinite sequence.

This book builds that space from the binary digits of a uniform number in $[0,1]$, and then stays inside what the construction can prove. The law of large numbers, the central limit theorem, the convergence of a finite Markov chain, a martingale stopped under a stated bound, and a Brownian path are theorems about that model. The Gaussian integral of Volume~1 becomes the density of one normal law, and then the shape of a sum of many small independent pieces. The last chapter uses an average to prove that a colouring or a graph exists, without exhibiting one.

Nothing in a proof is borrowed from a later volume. When an argument needs an iterated integral, a tail bound, or an infinite sequence of normal variables, the chapter supplies it. Exercises are graded \textbf{$\star$} for a short check, \textbf{$\star\star$} for a serious sitting, and \textbf{$\star\star\star$} for a harder synthesis. Each one has a hint and a complete solution. A ``Pause and think'' box is a guess to make before the next paragraph.

The series asks how far order and chance can be told apart. The primes are not yet these coins. Volume~5, \emph{Calculation: From Formula to Insight}, takes up the generating function as a series, with a remainder you can bound.
