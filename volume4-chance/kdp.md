# Description

A probability is a model you write down before you compute. This book builds one sample space that holds an infinite sequence of tosses, starting from the finite experiments of Volume 1.

You will meet the ballot theorem, Hoeffding's inequality, the central limit theorem, the law of large numbers, Markov chains, martingales, Brownian motion, and the probabilistic method. The law of large numbers is the fact that frequencies settle. The central limit theorem, which de Moivre found for coin tosses, is why a sum of many small independent pieces has a Gaussian law. A simple random walk on the line returns to its start; in dimension three or more it may leave forever. A Markov chain remembers only its present state. The top-to-random shuffle and the riffle shuffle say how many steps make a deck close to uniform. The Poisson law of rare events covers counts such as Bortkiewicz's record of Prussian cavalry kicked by horses. The Poisson process is the clock for arrivals, built from exponential waits. A martingale is a fair game, and optional stopping shows why a doubling strategy fails when the clock is not bounded. Brownian motion is the continuous path built here from normal increments. The probabilistic method uses a random choice to prove that a definite object exists.

Every step used in a proof is proved in this book or in Volume 1. Where a later volume owns a stronger theorem, such as Lévy's continuity theorem or Itô's formula, the text names it and does not use it as a premise. Each chapter has graded exercises, each with a hint and a complete solution, together with a short list of sources.

Written for curious minds with a taste for rigour, who read mathematics for the pleasure of understanding it.
