# Description

A pollen grain jiggling in water, a stock price, the spectrum of a heavy nucleus and the zeros of the Riemann zeta function all move to the same music: Brownian motion. This book builds it, develops the calculus it requires, puts it to work across analysis, networks and random matrices, and follows it all the way to the zeros of zeta. Every theorem is proved in full.

The first part builds Brownian motion and its calculus. Gaussian processes and Kolmogorov's continuity criterion give the path; Lévy's modulus of continuity, the zero set of dimension one half and the law of the maximum describe its fine structure. Continuous martingales and the Itô integral lead to Itô's formula, Lévy's characterisation, the Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz time change, Tanaka's formula and the local time. Brownian motion then solves the heat equation through the Feynman-Kac formula, changes probability through Girsanov's theorem, solves the Dirichlet problem, decides which dimensions are recurrent, and in the plane is invariant under conformal maps.

The second part puts random motion to work. Stochastic differential equations go from Ornstein-Uhlenbeck and Black-Scholes to Bessel processes and Dyson's Brownian motion of eigenvalues, with the Euler-Maruyama scheme to solve them numerically. Markov semigroups, Poisson random measures and the Lévy-Itô decomposition describe every process with independent increments. Random walks on networks become electrical circuits, recurrence is decided by resistance, and Kesten's theorem links random walks on groups to amenability. Expander graphs, the most efficient networks there are, save random bits, and satisfy their own Riemann hypothesis through Ihara's zeta function, with Ramanujan graphs as the extremal case.

The third part is about random matrices. Wigner's semicircle law, the Marchenko-Pastur law and the phase transition of a signal hidden in noise come first. Then the unitary group: the eigenvalues of a random unitary matrix follow the sine kernel, their counts are sums of independent coin flips, and the characteristic polynomial splits into independent factors, which give the moments found by Keating and Snaith and a central limit theorem for its logarithm. These are the laws that Montgomery, Dyson and Odlyzko found in the zeros of the Riemann zeta function. The book ends with log-correlated fields, the branching random walk and Kahane's multiplicative chaos, and with the conjecture of Fyodorov, Hiary and Keating on the largest values of zeta.

Every result is proved, with two clearly marked exceptions at the research frontier. Each chapter opens with the story behind its ideas, checked against the original sources, and has pause-and-think questions, figures, and graded exercises, each with a hint and a complete solution that first explains how to find the idea.

Every chapter comes with a Python notebook that checks the numbers in the text and opens a window on computer science and statistics: fractal dimensions and long memory, anytime-valid A/B tests, hedging with finitely many trades, walk-on-spheres solvers, sampling stable laws, graph sparsification, expander walks, principal component analysis in high dimension, ten thousand zeros of the zeta function computed from scratch, and the height of random binary search trees.

Written for curious minds with a taste for rigour, who read mathematics for the pleasure of understanding it.
