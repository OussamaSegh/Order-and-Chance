\chapter*{About the Cover}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{About the Cover}
\noindent Two hundred simple random walks, two of them drawn in a stronger line, the curves at height twice the square root of the number of steps, and a Gaussian profile standing at the right-hand end. George Pólya proved in 1921 that a walk on the line or the plane returns to its starting point with probability one, while a walk in space may leave forever. The envelope is the scale of the central limit theorem, which this volume sharpens, and the returns are taken up again in that chapter's account of recurrence. The drawing was computed by the book's own TikZ cover generator.
