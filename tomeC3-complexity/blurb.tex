Can every precise question be answered by a machine? Can every true statement be proved? And among the questions a
computer can answer, which can it answer quickly? Turing, G\"odel and Cook turned these questions into theorems, and
this book proves them.

Along the way: finite automata and regular expressions, grammars and parsing, Turing machines and the halting
problem, Post's correspondence problem, tilings and Hilbert's tenth problem, the $\lambda$-calculus and the
correspondence between proofs and programs, completeness and incompleteness, Goodstein's theorem, time and space
hierarchies, NP-completeness from Cook--Levin to Karp's reductions, the polynomial hierarchy and counting,
randomness, interactive proofs and zero knowledge, circuit lower bounds and proof complexity.

Every theorem is proved in full or clearly labelled as quoted, every exercise has a hint and a reasoned solution, and
every chapter comes with a Python notebook that builds the machines and runs them. Written for the pleasure of
seeing why some things cannot be done.
